## Ten sources to start with

1. **DJ Screw: A Life in Slow Revolution**, Lance Scott Walker, 2022 (01 #1). The fullest life of Screw, told by the people around him; the first book for Sloura's backstory and voice.
2. **DJ Screw: A Fast Life in Slow Motion**, Walker, *RBMA Daily*, 2015 (01 #8). How the technique worked and where it came from, up to the making of "June 27th". Free to read.
3. **The Slow Life and Fast Death of DJ Screw**, Michael Hall, *Texas Monthly*, April 2001 (01 #6). The defining account written months after his death: how he lived and how he died.
4. **Brothers in Rhyme, "DJ Screw" page**, University of Houston Libraries (01 #32). The gray tapes as objects (100-minute gray Maxells, hand-written labels, no track lists); a direct design reference.
5. **3 'N the Mornin' (Part Two)**, DJ Screw, 1995 (01 #36). The usual first listen. Read it with the Crack Magazine essay on it (01 #11).
6. **Remembering Michael "5000" Watts**, *Texas Standard*, 2026 (01 #20). The clearest statement of south side against north side: cassette against CD, radio and internet.
7. **Future Screw Crew Tumblr**, 2013–2016 (02 #1). The crew's own record, read in full: 249 posts, members listed with HeDawn, and the lean vocabulary in its tags.
8. **OG Ron C on the evolution of slowed and chopped music**, *Reverb*, 2020 (02 #14). The source for "chopped not slopped", the craft standard Sloura sets itself against.
9. **How Slowed + Reverb Remixes Became the Melancholy Heart of Music YouTube**, Andy Cush, *Pitchfork*, 2020 (03 #1). The fullest account of where slowed + reverb started, and of the big channels not mentioning "Screw or Houston".
10. **"'Slowed + Reverb' is just chopped & screwed gentrified"**, Shelby Stewart, *Houston Chronicle*, 2020 (03 #2). The Houston voice in the critique Sloura will meet when it is heard as slowed + reverb.

## Still thin

Each gap below is named in the lists themselves.

- **Primary audio.** No official or licensed stream of the gray tapes was checked; the Internet Archive copies are fan uploads (01, "What is thin").
- **Swishahouse in its own voice.** Only the label's About page and a 2013 panel report; no long interview with Watts (01, "What is thin").
- **Peer-reviewed scholarship.** One drug-education paper and one paper of unknown venue; JSTOR and Project MUSE were not tried (01, "What is thin"; 01 #27, #28).
- **Women in the scene.** No source centres them; "My Life with DJ Screw" by Nikki Williams (2011) is in the UH finding aid but was not opened (01, "What is thin").
- **The crew beyond its own pages.** No press besides Green Label, no interviews with El Blanco Nino or Minimz, and #CODEINEFOLK found nowhere outside the crew's X bio (02 E). Trill Scott Heron's *Luv Is Screwed Up EP* is unreachable; Hedawn may have a copy (02 #9). HeDawn's own Tumblr was read only on its front page (02 #11).
- **Books named but not opened.** Grafton Tanner's *Babbling Corpse* (vaporwave) and Jia Tolentino's *Trick Mirror* chapter on DJ Screw (03 E, 03 #4).
- **Open conflicts.** Watts's age at death, 52 or 54 (01 #20); the *Slowed and Throwed* exhibition dates, 2020 or 2021 (01 #35); Wikipedia's 2006 Kafan origin for slowed + reverb (03 #7); the Kundera line on slowness and memory, not found and not to be quoted (03 #27).
