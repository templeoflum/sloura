# Reference library 3: the wider culture of slowness (slowed + reverb, vaporwave, time-stretch art)

Board task T-0658. Gathered 14 Oct 2026 from public pages, no logins. Every URL below was opened in this run. The copy of each page (or the failure) is in `evidence/t0658/`, `evidence/t0658/README.md` maps each entry to its saved file, and `fetch-log.tsv` gives the HTTP result and time of each fetch. Sources already in library 2 (Clash on slowed + reverb, Know Your Meme, Eccojams, witch house) are not repeated. Entries that build on them point to "02 #n".

**Orientation: how each thread relates to Houston chopped and screwed**
1. **Slowed + reverb (A):** Pitchfork dates it to a 2017 upload by Slater, who is "from Houston" and "grew up … listening to DJ Screw", and says his style lacks Screw's "chopped" beats. It spread on YouTube, Spotify and TikTok, where, per Pitchfork, the big channels don't mention "Screw or Houston". In Aug 2020 the Houston Chronicle and The Root called it "gentrified" chopped and screwed (2, 3). *Reading:* that backlash is the argument Sloura's "high-fidelity chopped and screwed" claim walks into.
2. **Vaporwave (B):** Adam Harper's 2012 essay describes vaporwave slowing pop and corporate music down, "making it, as the term goes, 'screwed'", and Wikipedia describes a Floral Shoppe track as "slowed down, chopped and screwed". *Reading:* it is the nearest cousin to a Weave: slow, sample-built and nostalgic, with no rap.
3. **The tags (C):** a 2010 review calls drag/screwgaze/witch house a "progression of … chopped and screwed"; Wikipedia lists chopped and screwed and DJ Screw among phonk's origins and says drift phonk "became conflated with the original phonk genre". On Bandcamp, "drift" on its own tags mostly ambient releases, not slowed music. *Reading:* these are genres that start from Screw and then drift away from him.
4. **Time-stretch art (D):** classical works, tape loops, film and software stretched until duration itself is the subject. None of the saved copies for entries 15–23 mentions DJ Screw or chopped and screwed (text search of the saved copies; the only "Houston" is a Houston Chronicle citation on the Basinski page). *Reading:* it offers Sloura a "philology" lineage alongside Houston's, not descended from it.
5. **Writing on slowness and listening (E):** deep listening, hauntology and lost futures, Kundera's novel on speed and slowness, and one lab study of reverb and nostalgia. None of these saved sources mentions DJ Screw or chopped and screwed; the one Houston tie is biographical (Oliveros "attend[ed] the University of Houston", per Wikipedia). *Reading:* these give the bio's "study of slowness" a vocabulary, and the link to Houston would be Sloura's own.

Labels: **[verified]** means checked against the saved copy in this run. **[unverified]** means the page could not confirm the point, or it rests on one weak source. *Reading:* marks my interpretation of what a source means for Sloura; it is not a claim the source makes. Text in quotation marks is copied from the saved copies. "Via Wikipedia" means the work itself was not opened, only its encyclopedia article.

## A. Slowed + reverb on YouTube, Spotify and TikTok, and the argument over its roots

1. **"How Slowed + Reverb Remixes Became the Melancholy Heart of Music YouTube"** by Andy Cush, *Pitchfork*, 7 Apr 2020. *Type: magazine feature (with interviews).*
   Where: https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/how-slowed-reverb-remixes-became-the-melancholy-heart-of-music-youtube/
   Why it matters: The fullest account of the style's start found in this run. "In 2017, Jarylun Moore uploaded his second-ever video to YouTube: a homemade remix of Lil Uzi Vert's '20 Minutes'". Moore (Slater) "is from Houston, where he grew up … listening to DJ Screw". His style lacks the "disorienting 'chopped' beats". He is quoted: "the chops are sacred to the culture, and not everybody can imitate it." It also notes that neither Slater nor the larger channel Rum World mentions "Screw or Houston" on their channels. *Reading:* that gap is where Sloura would stand. [verified]

2. **"'Slowed + Reverb' is just chopped & screwed gentrified"** by Shelby Stewart, *Houston Chronicle* (chron.com), 13 Aug 2020 (updated 17 Aug). *Type: opinion column (critique).*
   Where: https://www.chron.com/entertainment/article/Slowed-Reverb-is-just-chopped-screwed-15482351.php
   Why it matters: It is the Houston voice in the critique: "Give DJ Screw his flowers. Slowed + reverb is a poor imitation of what chopped and screwed music is." It also says: "Slowed + reverb is not something that's new. And guess what? It's Black." *Reading:* anyone who hears Sloura as slowed + reverb may raise this argument too. [verified]

3. **"DJ Screw's Legacy Is Being Celebrated After TikTok Teens Tried Gentrifying His 'Chopped and Screwed' Style"** by J'na Jefferson, *The Root* (The Grapevine), Aug 2020 (the tweets it embeds are dated 13 Aug 2020; the saved copy shows no publication date). *Type: article (critique).*
   Where: https://www.theroot.com/dj-screws-legacy-is-being-celebrated-after-tiktok-teens-1844726412
   Why it matters: It reports the TikTok incident: the 80–90% speed claim against Houston's "60 and 70" BPM, and Slater's replies: "I will pay you $3,000 to have this scrubbed from the internet" and "i am and what I created are 10000% inspired and influenced by dj screw". It closes by asking people to leave hip-hop alone "without gentrifiers and cultural erasure". [verified for the page text. Publication day **unverified**]

4. **"TikToker Dev Lemons Faces Backlash for Calling Chopped and Screwed 'Slowed and Reverb'"** by Gina Vaynshteyn, *Distractify*, 13 Aug 2020. *Type: article.*

5. **"Lo-Fi Hip-Hop Has Become One Of The Internet's Most Popular Subgenres; Is Slowed & Reverb Next?"** by Elijah C. Watson, *Okayplayer*, 17 Nov 2020. *Type: article.*

[verified]

## B. Vaporwave: "screwed" pop without Houston

    Where: the live address (dummymag.com, now dmy.co) gives 404. Internet Archive copy: https://web.archive.org/web/20131230184500/http://www.dummymag.com/features/adam-harper-vaporwave
    Why it matters: An early critical essay on vaporwave (Wikipedia's Floral Shoppe article cites it), and it uses Houston's word: vaporwave slows its material down, "making it, as the term goes, 'screwed'".     Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_Shoppe
    Why it matters: Wikipedia: it "is frequently cited as the first proper example" of vaporwave. ## D. Time-stretch art: slowness as the work itself

15. **9 Beet Stretch** by Leif Inge, 2002 (realised at NOTAM, Oslo; concert premiere 16–17 Apr 2004, Madison, Wisconsin). *Type: sound work (24-hour electroacoustic piece).* Sources: **"Closer to Eternity: Stretching Beethoven's 9th"**, NPR *All Things Considered*, 26 Nov 2002, and the Wikipedia article.
    Where: https://www.npr.org/2002/11/26/858257/closer-to-eternity-stretching-beethovens-9th · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_Beet_Stretch
    Why it matters: NPR: "Inge took the hour-long symphony and stretched it to last an entire 24 hours". Wikipedia adds "with no pitch distortions". Screw, by contrast, worked by "dramatically reducing the pitch of a record" (Wikipedia, saved as `wiki-slowedreverb.html`). *Reading:* a "slow it until you hear inside it" piece and a strong ancestor for "sonic philology". [verified]

16. **Paul's Extreme Sound Stretch (Paulstretch)** by Nasca Octavian Paul, free software (GPL), page snapshot 20 Dec 2015. *Type: software and its documentation.*
    Where: live page 403. Internet Archive copy: https://web.archive.org/web/20151220133340/http://hypermammut.sourceforge.net/paulstretch/ · source code: https://github.com/paulnasca/paulstretch_cpp
    Why it matters: The maker's own description: "suitable only for extreme sound stretching of the audio (like 50x)", and "the stretch amount is unlimited". Entry 17 shows it used on a Justin Bieber song. *Reading:* worth knowing as a technique distinct from varispeed screwing (my understanding is that it keeps pitch; how it treats drums and transients is **unverified** here). [verified via the Wayback copy]

17. **"U Smile 800% Slower"** by Shamantis (Nick Pittsinger), Aug 2010, a Paulstretch of Justin Bieber's "U Smile". Source: **"How It Works: The Art Of Time-Stretching Bieber"** by Sami Yenigun, NPR *The Record*, 18 Aug 2010. *Type: track (online upload), with an explainer article.*
    Where: https://www.npr.org/sections/therecord/2010/08/18/129283985/the-art-of-a-time-stretch
    Why it matters: Seven years before Slater's 2017 upload, a 3:17 pop song was made to last "over 35 minutes". "Pittsinger says he used a program called PaulStretch". *Reading:* a line to the slowed-pop audience that does not run through Houston. Whether it was the first slowed pop edit to spread widely is **unverified**; NPR's saved text does not say so. (A New Statesman piece by Daniel Trilling, 9 Sep 2010, "The Art of Listening: Justin Bieber 800 per cent slower", was also saved, but only its first paragraph came through, so it is not counted.) A search summary said Wikipedia's "U Smile" article credits a different maker; that page was not opened, so the attribution conflict is **unverified**. [verified for the NPR text]

18. **Everywhere at the End of Time** by The Caretaker (James Leyland Kirby), six stages released 2016–2019. *Type: album series.* Via Wikipedia.
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everywhere_at_the_End_of_Time
    Why it matters: Its "degraded samples of old ballroom music" portray "the stages of Alzheimer's disease". It shows decay and slowness used as storytelling across a series released at fixed gaps ("after a six-month period for listeners to feel the passage of time"). *Reading:* a model for a numbered Weave series. It later became "a YouTube and TikTok phenomenon". [verified]

19. **"Leyland Kirby on The Caretaker's New Project: Six Albums Exploring Dementia"** by Andrew Parks, *Bandcamp Daily*, 17 Oct 2016. *Type: interview.*
    Where: https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/the-caretaker-leyland-kirby-interview
    Why it matters: The maker in his own words, at the start of the series. He compares himself with Basinski (his pieces "aren't just loops breaking down") and talks about method: "I go through thousands of tracks to find loops sometimes." *Reading:* a working artist's view on choosing source material, which is a Weave's first step. [verified]

20. **"'Everywhere At The End Of Time' Becomes TikTok Challenge"** by Patrick Clarke, *The Quietus*, 19 Oct 2020. *Type: news story with interview.*
    Where: https://thequietus.com/news/the-caretaker-tiktok-everywhere-at-the-end-of-time-interview/ (reached from thequietus.com/?p=29098)
    Why it matters: A six-and-a-half-hour work becomes "an unlikely viral hit" on TikTok two months after the slowed + reverb backlash (A, Aug 2020). Kirby on younger listeners: "shared experience is everything". He says the work "is not on Spotify" and that sales are what he survives on. *Reading:* a parallel for Sloura: a slow artist who lives on sales, not streams, meeting TikTok. [verified]

21. **The Disintegration Loops** by William Basinski, 2062 (label), 2002–2003 (four volumes; remastered box from Temporary Residence, 2012). *Type: album series.* Via Wikipedia.
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Disintegration_Loops
    Why it matters: "Made from looping magnetic tapes that sampled earlier recordings, each track's loop decays". Basinski "dedicated the series to the victims of the attacks" after watching the World Trade Center fall, and the page says it "is considered a significant work in ambient and electronic music". *Reading:* where and when a slow piece was made becomes part of what it means. That is philology. [verified]

22. **24 Hour Psycho** by Douglas Gordon, 1993 (Tramway, Glasgow, and Kunst-Werke, Berlin). *Type: video installation.* Via Wikipedia.
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Hour_Psycho
    Why it matters: Hitchcock's *Psycho* is "slowed down to approximately two frames per second from its original 24". *Reading:* the visual counterpart to 9 Beet Stretch, useful for Sloura's visual side (covers, reels): slowing an image as a way to study it, not only to set a mood. [verified]

23. **Organ²/ASLSP (As Slow as Possible)**, Halberstadt performance, by John Cage (composition), Burchardi church, started 2001 and due to end in 2640. *Type: musical performance (ongoing).* Via Wikipedia.
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Slow_as_Possible
    Why it matters: *Reading:* the far end of the scale, Cage's instruction to play "as slow as possible", taken at its word. The page counts "25 years, 1 month, 1 week and 1 day" so far. It is also a reference point and a possible caption source for "the study of slowness". [verified]

## E. Writing on slowness and listening

24. **Deep Listening: A Composer's Sound Practice** by Pauline Oliveros, iUniverse, 2005 (ISBN 978-0-595-34365-2). *Type: book.* Via Wikipedia (Pauline Oliveros article; "Deep Listening" redirects there).
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Listening
    Why it matters: Oliveros "coined the term 'deep listening'" in 1988 after descending into an underground cistern "to make a recording", and the Deep Listening Band "specialized in performing and recording in resonant or reverberant spaces such as caves, cathedrals and huge underground cisterns". She studied at the University of Houston (per the article). *Reading:* a listening practice born in reverb, useful for the reverb half of the debate. The book itself was not opened. [verified for the citation and the article text]

25. **Ghosts of My Life: Writings on Depression, Hauntology and Lost Futures** by Mark Fisher, Zero Books, 2014. *Type: book (essays).* Via Wikipedia (the title redirects to the Mark Fisher article).
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghosts_of_My_Life
    Why it matters: Wikipedia: the book "examined the idea [of hauntology] through cultural sources including the music of Burial, Joy Division, and the Ghost Box label". *Reading,* not checked against the book: Sloura's slowness is likely to be read through this lens, as The Caretaker is (see 26). The book itself was not opened. [verified for the article text]

26. **Hauntology (music)** by Wikipedia contributors, current revision. *Type: encyclopedia article.*
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauntology_(music)
    Why it matters: It traces the term from Derrida (1993) through Ian Penman (1995), Simon Reynolds and Mark Fisher, and names "The Caretaker", "William Basinski" and "Burial". *Reading:* it links threads D and E and points to the critics who wrote about decayed, looped sound outside Houston. [verified]

27. **Slowness** (*La Lenteur*) by Milan Kundera, 1995 in French (English 1996, Harper Perennial per Wikipedia). *Type: novel.* Via Wikipedia.
    Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slowness_(novel)
    Why it matters: A short novel in which, per Wikipedia, "the speed of modern living is the key concept", used to "expound his philosophical ideas about modernity, technology, memory and sensuality". The often-quoted line that slowness is "proportional to the intensity of memory" was **not** found in the saved page, so it is **unverified** and must not be quoted until checked in the book. [verified for the article text]

28. **"Reverberation time and musical emotion in recorded music listening"** by H. Wilkie and P. M. C. Harrison, *Music Perception*, 2025 (doi 10.1525/mp.2025.2333445). *Type: academic paper.*
    Where: https://cms.mus.cam.ac.uk/publications/wilkie-reverberation/ (Cambridge Centre for Music and Science listing with abstract. The paper itself was not opened.)
    Why it matters: The only experimental study found in this run on what reverb does to listeners: "longer RTs had a significant positive effect on aspects of 'Sublimity' (i.e., 'Nostalgia,' 'Transcendence,' 'Wonder')". It tested reverberation time on recorded music over headphones; it does not test slowing or slowed + reverb. *Reading:* it may bear on why the "+ reverb" half of the style is heard as nostalgic; that link is mine, not the paper's. [verified for the abstract]
