'Slowed + Reverb' is just chopped & screwed gentrified

By Shelby Stewart, Digital reporterUpdated Aug 17, 2020 10:23 a.m.

I'd like to argue that Houston is one of the most disrespected cities when it comes to music industry. We never get the credit we're owed for the trends we've set, or the styles we created in music.

The latest crime? "Slowed + reverb." The phenomenon, discovered by suburban white teens, is a simple DIY remixing style that has thrived on YouTube in recent years, according to Pitchfork.

But at the root of this so called "slowed + reverb" is a very familiar sound-- chopped and screwed, birthed right here in Houston, Texas. The phenomenon has become more and more popular, and gives credit to the artist Slater, for creating such an innovate sound.

I'm not sure who Slater is, but I'd like a word with him.

To increase the level of disrespect, there's a viral Tik Tok video explaining the slowed + reverb concept, that's been floating around on Twitter.

— Gab (@netfreak_gab) August 12, 2020

The video infuriated many Houstonians, including myself. The video received so much backlash, that it caused DJ Screw to trend.

Beyond the Tik Tok video not acknowledging DJ Screw, it bothers me that there are artists who believe that they've suddenly created a new genre, as if it didn't already exist.

That's just Texas.

People defend slowed + reverb as if it was inspired by chopped and screwed, but doesn't give DJ Screw his proper acclaim. Slowed + reverb is not something that's new. And guess what? It's Black. Let's give chopped and screwed music its props. Give DJ Screw his flowers. Slowed + reverb is a poor imitation of what chopped and screwed music is. Just put in a Screw Tape.
